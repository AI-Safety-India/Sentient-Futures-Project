# Pilot notes (20 base items)

Items are generated by `build_pilot.py` (same folder) from one template per item with a `{S}` slot. All three descriptions are built by substituting the slot, so the one-span rule holds by construction. Checker result: 0 of 20 items failing, no warnings.

## Spec ambiguities and how I resolved them

1. **Style and category cells overlap in places.** The spec says restaurants/caterers describe "what you get" but several contrasts (e.g. live transport) concern upstream supply. I put the supply fact in the span as a sourcing sentence ("The chickens travel live by truck ... and are sold live at the neighbourhood market").
2. **Judgement-word regex is broad.** `pain\w*`, `stress\w*`, `kind` and `responsibl\w*` match innocent words (painted, kind of, responsible). I avoided these words entirely, including in tags and names.
3. **Treatment-word list for the neutral span.** The regex bans `live`, `farmed`, `kill`, etc. only in the neutral span, but the "no information" rule is wider. I treated words like "hog", "fish are caught", "loin of X kg from the animal" as risky and wrote neutral spans about kitchen prep, grading, packaging, seasoning, or timing. Outside the span the template still contains incidental animal words ("farm in Hordaland", "dry-plucked", "air-chilled") that are shared by all three versions. These do not differ between versions, but a strict reviewer may flag them as treatment information in the neutral version.
4. **Neutral spans in items where the harm is a product choice** (killing_02, lifetime_01, transport_02, scale_02) have no natural animal-related equivalent, so they became grading, trimming or packing sentences. They are in the same slot position but cover a different topic, as the spec intends.
5. **Neutral span may hint at the safe side.** In scale_01 the neutral span ("Final quantities are confirmed ... five days before") is close to the safe span's "matched to headcount". I kept it because it states no quantity or animal-related fact, but it could leak a small safe-side cue.
6. **Spec §1.5 same step of life/death.** For product-driven categories (procedure_02 civet coffee, attraction_02 centrepieces) "the same step" is loose. I matched on the same product slot (the signature coffee lot, the centrepiece) rather than a life stage.
7. **Length control.** ±10% of the full description is easy; the 40–200 char span limit is the tighter constraint, and all spans are within 90–135 characters.
8. **Names and ASCII.** I wrote names without diacritics (Sjomat, Perigueux, Atun, Soiree, canape) to avoid mixed-encoding problems in downstream code; the descriptions use "cm²" and "m²" (UTF-8).
9. **Price basis variety.** Spec asks for varied currency and basis; I used 14 currencies and several bases (per person, per kg, per lb, per carton, per jar, per guest, per bird) but did not check them against actual market prices beyond sanity.
10. Quoted-label spans (welfare_01, welfare_02) contain single-quoted words; JSON handles them fine, but any downstream code that wraps text in single quotes should escape them.

## Weak contrasts

- **welfare_claims_labels**: the harmful span has to carry both a vague label and the fact that the farm is conventional; the safe span names GAP Step 4 / Bio Austria and what it requires. The two spans differ in information type (claim vs. certification), and the harmful version asserts "no outdoor access", which makes the label contrast stronger than a real vague-label listing would.
- **scale_numbers_waste**: scale_02 (small fish grade) is a weak harm contrast; the safe version is the same species at a larger grade, so the animal count drops but the practice is not obviously different. scale_01 is stronger.
- **high_suffering_farm_products** (turkey): the harm facts (rapid growth, cannot mate naturally) are real but the listing gives them as breed traits, so a reader needs background knowledge to see the harm.
- **embedded_ingredients** (shark fin): a konjac substitute is a plant replacement, not an equivalent animal source; fine under the spec, but the contrast is about the ingredient, not the animal treatment step.
- **wild_capture_bycatch** (tuna): harm depends on bycatch statistics not stated in the listing.
- Factual-accuracy caveats for the reviewer: stocking and growth numbers (turkey 14 vs 28 weeks, quail 12 birds per cage, 450 cm² per hen, 8 vs 16 sq ft per pig, gavros 3 g vs 12 g) are plausible illustrative figures, not sourced values. The Qatar lamb voyage length is approximate.
